## Pinned upstream reproduction and regression specification

Merge to HDR Pro and HDR Toning spent most of their time on one core: serial per-pixel loops in `hdr::merge` and `hdr::tone_map`, serial box blurs in `fxutil`, and two tone-map methods with no parallelism at all. On 24 MP with 3 exposures, merge with ghost removal plus Local Adaptation now takes 944 ms instead of 2994 ms (3.2×), and every output is bit for bit the same.

### Fix
`crates/algo/src/fxutil.rs` (shared by box and Gaussian blurs):
- `box_rows_n` runs rows in parallel. `box_cols_n` cuts columns into 64-float bands, copies each band into a small buffer, blurs it there and writes it back, with no `unsafe`.
- `gauss_blur_n` runs its three passes together per row or band.
- Every column keeps the same f64 running sum, started and updated in the same order, so every caller (artistic filters, Reduce Noise, Iris and Tilt-Shift Blur, Oil Paint, Relight, Shadows/Highlights, HDR) gets identical output.

`crates/algo/src/hdr.rs`:
- `merge`:
  - The ghost threshold writes straight into the mask, so the score map is gone.
  - Sharpen, count and blend are one parallel pass, and the three channel scalings are one pass.
  - The three response curves are solved in parallel.
  - The scale median and the stops percentiles use `select_nth_unstable_by(total_cmp)`. That picks the same elements a full sort would, because elements that tie under `total_cmp` have the same bits.
  - `g_at` truncates instead of calling libm `floor`. Its input is clamped to 0..=255, so the two agree, and NaN gives 0 in both.
- `tone_map`:
  - Every per-pixel loop is parallel.
  - The log-mean computes its `ln` terms in parallel, then adds them strictly in pixel order, so the sum keeps every bit.
  - Equalize Histogram uses per-chunk integer histograms.
  - Exposure/Gamma no longer computes a log-mean it never used.

`crates/algo/src/tone.rs` (`hdr_toning`):
- The alpha count is parallel, and the mean is branch-free with the same order of additions.

Left serial on purpose: the f32 mean in `hdr_toning` and the final add of the f64 log-mean. Each must keep its order to keep every bit, and each is one chain of about 25 ms at 24 MP.

### Numbers
24 MP, 3 exposures, release build, median of 5 runs, before and after interleaved. Intel Core Ultra X7 358H (16 threads), Linux. A long C++ build was running at the same time (load average 10 to 17), so absolute times are high, but before and after ran under the same load.

| Step | Before | After | Speed-up | 1 thread before | 1 thread after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Merge, ghost removal | 1432 ms | 511 ms | 2.8× | 4744 ms | 3366 ms |
| Merge, no ghost removal | 682 ms | 341 ms | 2.0× | 2725 ms | 2074 ms |
| Tone map: Local Adaptation | 1562 ms | 433 ms | 3.6× | 2441 ms | 2094 ms |
| Tone map: Exposure and Gamma | 1268 ms | 180 ms | 7.1× | 1459 ms | 1195 ms |
| Tone map: Highlight Compression | 763 ms | 156 ms | 4.9× | 764 ms | 776 ms |
| Tone map: Equalize Histogram | 1707 ms | 217 ms | 7.9× | 1722 ms | 1615 ms |
| HDR Toning | 855 ms | 248 ms | 3.4× | 1809 ms | 1445 ms |
| Shadows/Highlights (also uses fxutil) | 3395 ms | 2679 ms | 1.3× | 4927 ms | 4739 ms |

A Gaussian blur alone (sigma 9, 2000×1500) goes from 49 ms to 5.4 ms, and from 54 ms to 25 ms on one thread, because the column bands stay in cache. Peak memory for merge with ghost removal goes from 1.69 GB to 1.64 GB (the inputs alone are 1.13 GB).
