Part of #6379 (the multi-page TIFF part).

File › Import › Video Frames to Layers loads every frame of an animated GIF as its own layer, but a multi-page TIFF gave only its first page. `load_frames` (also behind Layer › Video Layers › New Video Layer from File) now takes every full-resolution page of a TIFF's main IFD chain, in file order, one layer (or frame) each:

- `tiff_pages` lists the pages through `codecs::tiff_info`: main-chain directories (`parent: None`) of kind `TiffPageKind::Page`. Thumbnails and other reduced-resolution copies, transparency masks and SubIFDs are left out.
- Each page opens through `io::import_tiff_page`, so its orientation is applied and a page with Photoshop layer data contributes its composite, as when that page is opened.
- A TIFF with one page goes through the usual import as before.

The change sits next to the GIF branch in `load_frames`. @LloydNicholson's #5635 (animated WebP) changes the same function; the two merge without a conflict in `video_cmds.rs`, and the new test sits apart from #5635's tests.

APNG frames and an option to open frames or pages straight away as layers (the rest of #6379) aren't part of this.

Test: `video_cmds::tests::video_frames_to_layers_takes_every_tiff_page` writes a TIFF with a thumbnail and two pages of different sizes and grey levels: Video Frames to Layers gives exactly two layers, "Frame 1" 6×4 and "Frame 2" 3×5, with each page's grey, and a single-page TIFF still gives one layer. It fails on main (one layer). The other `video_cmds` tests pass (30).
