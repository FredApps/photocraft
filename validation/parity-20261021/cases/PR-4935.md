- Opened duotones show their inks, not just the grey plate.

## The block

The PSD specification leaves it undocumented. Photoshop 2025 writes 524 bytes; I made monotone, duotone, tritone and quadtone files with known inks in Photoshop and read the layout from them:

| offset | size | content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2 | version, 1 |
| 2 | 2 | ink count, 1–4 |
| 4 | 4 × 10 | ink colours, as in swatch files (`AcoColor`); space 0xFFFF = no ink |
| 44 | 4 × 64 | ink names, Pascal strings |
| 300 | 4 × 28 | curves: 13 × i16 then an i16 0, the PSD's transfer-function layout |
| 412 | 2 | dot gain, 20 |
| 414 | 11 × 10 | overprint colours, 0xFFFF = computed |

A curve's 13 values are the ink density in tenths of a percent at gray densities of 0, 5, 10, 20, …, 90, 95 and 100 %, and −1 leaves a point out. I checked the positions in Photoshop. PhotoCraft wrote a black monotone whose curve is 0 up to 60 % density and 100 % at 100 %, and Photoshop, after converting it to RGB, keeps it white exactly up to 60.4 % density.

## Changes

- **`duotone_map.rs` (new):**
  - `parse` reads the inks: RGB, CMYK, Lab and gray colours shown in sRGB, plus names and curves.
  - `write` makes the block from `Duotone::inks`. It writes RGB colours. A curve whose points lie on the 13-point grid keeps exactly those points; any other curve is sampled at all 13. It also writes the default dot gain and computed overprints.
- **Import:** interprets the inks. A colour-book ink can't be shown here, so it shows as black with a warning. The block is still kept raw (`psd_raw`) and written back unchanged while the inks are unedited, as before.
- **Export:** a Duotone without a raw block gets one from `write`, so every 8/16-bit Duotone is written as Duotone.

Photoshop's own conversion of a duotone to RGB uses dot gain and overprint colours that PhotoCraft's preview doesn't model yet. Colours on screen therefore differ from Photoshop's RGB conversion (by up to about 30 levels for a black and orange duotone with curves). That is the display model, not the file, and I've left it for later.
