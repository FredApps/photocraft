Closes #7655.

⌘-clicking a layer thumbnail loads the layer's transparency as a partial selection, correctly. But dragging that selection with the Move tool left a faint ghost of the soft pixels behind, as in the issue's second screenshot.

The Move tool floats the selected pixels (`select.float` → `Floating::new`). For each pixel it lifted `a·k` and left `a·(1 − k)`, where `a` is the pixel's alpha and `k` the selection. With the selection made from the layer's own transparency, `k = a`, so every half-transparent pixel left `a·(1 − a)` behind (64 where `a` was 128). The lifted copy also came out fainter than the original.

### Photoshop, measured

All on macOS 27 with Photoshop 2025, exported with Quick Export as PNG and read pixel by pixel.

**The issue's case.** I used a layer with a red block whose alpha ramps from 0 to 255, ⌘-clicked its thumbnail, and dragged with the Move tool.
- Nothing is left where it was.
- The moved pixels keep their alpha: 2, 9, 64, 128, 192, 252, the same as the original.

**A case that separates the rules.** Layer B is a uniform 50 % red fill (alpha 128). The selection is another layer's ramp transparency (`k`). I moved B and read the residue left behind:

| `k` | 0.035 | 0.13 | 0.25 | 0.38 | **0.50** | 0.63 | 0.75 | 0.88 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Photoshop leaves | 121 | 100 | 72 | 38 | **0** | 6 | 8 | 6 |
| `a·(1 − k)`, before | 123 | 111 | 96 | 80 | 64 | 48 | 32 | 16 |
| `max(a − k, 0)`, this PR | 119 | 94 | 64 | 32 | **0** | 0 | 0 | 0 |

In every column, what Photoshop lifts plus what it leaves equals the pixel's alpha. It leaves nothing once the selection covers the pixel's own coverage. So Photoshop lifts about `min(a, k)`, not `a·k`.

The coverage rule is exact when `k = a` (this issue) and for opaque pixels. Elsewhere it is within 8 levels of Photoshop; the product was up to 64 levels off.

### Change

- **`float_cmds::Floating::new`:** the piece takes `min(a, k)` of a pixel's alpha, and the layer keeps `max(a − k, 0)`.
- **Opaque pixels split as before.** For `a = 1`, `min(1, k) = k` and `1 − k`, so a feathered move of an opaque layer is unchanged.
- **Also unchanged:** a copy (⌥) still leaves the original, and the Background's hole still takes the background colour.

Not changed: Free Transform with a selection (`transform_cmds::split_selected`) still uses the product. I haven't measured Photoshop there.

### Tests

- New: `a_layers_own_transparency_floats_its_soft_pixels_whole` (float_cmds/tests). It checks that:
  - a half-transparent area floated by its own transparency leaves alpha 0 behind and arrives at 0.5;
  - an opaque area moves whole;
  - a half selection of an opaque pixel still splits it 0.5 / 0.5.

  It fails without the fix, because 0.25 is left behind.
- `cargo fmt`, `clippy -D warnings`, the engine suite (2,015 tests), `panic_hunt -- --ignored`, the release build, `cargo xtask wasm` and `cargo xtask layers` pass. I ran these in this branch's own target directory.

### Verified in the app

On macOS 27 with a release build, using the same files as in Photoshop. In each case I loaded the selection, dragged with the Move tool, deselected and exported.

- **Ramp, before the fix:** a pink ghost, alpha up to 64.
- **Ramp, with the fix:** no residue, and the moved alpha is 2, 9, 34, 64, 128, 192, 252, as in Photoshop.
- **Two-layer case, with the fix:** the residue is the bottom row of the table, within 8 levels of Photoshop and exact at `k = a`.
