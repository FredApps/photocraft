Fixes #7289

### Problem
A background job (a filter started with `Session::start`, as the app runs Gaussian Blur and other slow filters) journals its step when its result is applied, not when it starts. An action that started recording in between got that step. For example:
- the job starts while recording A;
- A stops and B starts;
- the result lands, and B records a Gaussian Blur that was never run during it.

Replaying B then blurs. The same happened for a job started before any recording.

### Solution
A job remembers which recording it was started in, and only that recording gets its step.
- `ActionState::take` (`crates/engine/src/actions_cmds.rs`) counts recordings: each `actions.record` bumps it.
- `dispatch` (`crates/engine/src/jobs.rs`) stores the take that is recording when a job starts in `Running::recorded_by`. It does this once recording resumes after the command, so a job started while recording A is A's.
- `apply_job`: when another recording is running, its steps so far are committed first (`flush_recording`), and its start is moved past the job's journal entry. That recording keeps its steps before and after the result and leaves the job's out. A job started and applied in the same recording records as before.

As the issue leaves open, a job whose recording stopped before the result landed doesn't add a step to it afterwards.

### Tests
`jobs::tests::a_job_lands_in_the_recording_it_was_started_in_only`. Without the fix, the first case fails:
- **Started in A, applied after A stopped and B started:** A is empty. B holds only its own steps (`file.new` before the result, `desaturate` after it), not the blur.
- **Started before any recording, applied while B records:** B is empty.
- **Control, started and applied while A records:** A holds the blur.
